Identity & Access Management, in motion

Step through each simulation: how identities are verified, how entitlements flow, and how access is kept correct over time.

The IAM life cycle

IAM automates creating, provisioning, sharing, authenticating and updating identities. Maintenance is the stage that never ends: it loops back to keep access correct.

Entities that need digital identities

Example: Aisling, field technician. She is one person with many identities (employee, personal email, VoIP). Her laptop (MAC address, serial number), its operating system processes, installed applications and monitoring agents (EDR, antimalware) are each entities with their own identities. An agent is someone or something authorised to act on another's behalf, such as an assistant booking travel on a boss's card. They use their own identity but act with the boss's details. Linking identities to entities and permissions to identities forms the chain of trust.

IAM process: identity vs credential

Three parties take part: the claimant (user), the requesting system and the authenticating system.

Identity
Who or what you are: username, user ID. It differentiates entities.
Credential
Proof of the claim: something you know (password, PIN), have (token, smart card, certificate) or are (biometrics).

Entitlements and inherited rights

An entitlement is a specific permission: which resources a user may reach and what they may do. Pick a user and watch rights flow in through groups, roles and attributes instead of being assigned one by one.

Resulting entitlements

BasisMeaning
Role / responsibilityA manager sees confidential documents; regular staff do not.
Group membershipPermissions are applied to groups, not individuals.
Attributes (ABAC)Department, location or job title grant access dynamically.
Access control policiesRules state who may access what under which conditions.
Business needBalances security with productivity.

Inheritance: change a role once and every member inherits the change automatically. That is efficient, consistent and scalable.

How it works, where it is used

Pros
  • Central, consistent control of who can do what
  • Faster onboarding and offboarding
  • Least privilege reduces breach impact
  • Audit trails support compliance
  • Groups and roles scale to thousands of users
Cons
  • High setup and integration cost
  • Poor role design causes role explosion and privilege creep
  • The IAM system is itself a high-value target
  • MFA friction and lockouts affect users
  • Needs continuous reviews, which are often neglected
